# Seagrass Stories game
Source: OceanX, seagrassstoriesforschools.oceanx.org (Educator Guide, Investigate stage)

Seagrass Stories is an interactive web game. Learners play as marine scientist
Nada Abdelkader and help a struggling seagrass meadow recover. The game turns
ocean restoration into a hands-on challenge: learners clear rubbish and debris
from the seabed, place restoration mats, and track how the meadow changes.

Restoration mats support seagrass shoots so they can stay in place, take root
and grow. The game represents parts of the real restoration process, but not
all of it. Some things in the game change quickly, while the same change in
the real ocean can take much longer.

Driving question: What does it take to bring an underwater meadow back to life?

Learners play during the Investigate stage (15 minutes) and think about three
questions: What changes quickly? What might take longer in the ocean? What
evidence would tell you the meadow is recovering?

Evidence of learning: learners can tell the difference between an immediate
game response and real ecological change, and can name possible evidence that
a meadow is recovering.

Learning outcome (Think): recognise how restoration supports the recovery of
seagrass meadows, and how the game represents that process through changes,
timescales and evidence of recovery. Learning outcome (Feel): build curiosity
and a sense of agency, seeing how small, deliberate interventions can support
a habitat's recovery.

Seagrass meadows store carbon, produce oxygen, and support fisheries and
coastal protection. They are a blue carbon ecosystem.
